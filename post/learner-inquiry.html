8.1 Student Inquiry Project

Promoting learner inquiry and divergent thinking in the library.

Published on 23 October 2026 by Emily Fox

Project Overview

Using a modified Agile methodology, students will investigate how the school library’s collection, spaces, and services currently support learners and report back to the librarian where they could work better. Students will explore the collection, gather feedback, generate possibilities, test a practical idea, and/or share a recommendation with library staff in a continuous iteration/continuous delivery (CI/CD) cadence. The project is designed for self-directed participation during lunch, study hall, before or after school, or library passes. No scheduled lessons or direct instruction are required.

Driving Question

How might we make the school library’s collection, spaces, or services more useful, welcoming, and inspiring for the people who use them?

Learning Goals

Inquiry: Develop questions from observations, narrow an investigation, and use findings to make a recommendation.

Divergent thinking: Generate a wide range of ideas before selecting and refining the most valuable options.

Information literacy: Locate and evaluate relevant collection and user-feedback evidence; represent findings accurately and respectfully; learn the basics of a project management cadence.

Collaboration: Share responsibilities, listen to different perspectives, give feedback, and communicate group recommendations.

Program Resources

Students may visit at any time during open library access. Students can complete challenges individually or in groups, work asynchronously, and continue where they left off. Each phase’s instructions, prompts, templates, and checkpoint (when defined) are available through stations and the Google Classroom.

  • Stations will be placed in visible, accessible spots around the library. This will include a table nearest the door, the circulation desk, the back wall, and the study corrals/tables.
  • Google Classroomwill serve as a hub for the project, including surveys, prompts, templates, the Kanban board, a Miro board for brainstorming, etc.
  • QR codesthat link to Google Classroom will contain digital task cards, with short, self-explanatory steps, prompts, templates, and clear next actions. Students should be able to begin and continue without a staff-led explanation.
  • Project roadmapa Notion Kanban board where students can track progress and assign priority value to tasks.
  • Keep the staff role low-prep:restock station materials, offer light check-ins when students request them, and synthesize data into actionable steps (asynchronously).
